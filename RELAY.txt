==================================================
PRODUCT VISION
==================================================


RELAY is an AI-powered knowledge-transfer and workforce-localization platform.


Organizations around the world routinely bring experts into environments where specialized capabilities need to be developed.


Examples include:


- Peace Corps-style technical assistance;
- international development programs;
- expert secondments;
- multinational workforce localization;
- Saudization and national workforce-development initiatives;
- retiring employees transferring knowledge to successors;
- healthcare capacity building;
- manufacturing and industrial workforce development;
- infrastructure and energy projects;
- government capability-building;
- NGOs and humanitarian organizations.


The underlying problem is universal:


Organizations know how to move experts.


They are much less effective at systematically moving expertise.


An expert practitioner is not necessarily an expert:


- trainer;
- instructional designer;
- consultant;
- change manager;
- knowledge-management professional.


As a result:


- experts often perform work instead of transferring capability;
- teaching opportunities are missed;
- apprentices receive inconsistent exposure;
- tacit knowledge stays in the expert's head;
- knowledge is documented inconsistently;
- organizations track training rather than demonstrated capability;
- informal relationships and workarounds disappear;
- management cannot determine whether the organization can operate without the expert;
- valuable expertise may leave when the expert leaves.


RELAY provides the structure around the human expert.


RELAY DOES NOT REPLACE THE TRAINER.


It makes human-to-human knowledge transfer:


STRUCTURED
MEASURABLE
ACTIVE
PERSISTENT
SCALABLE


Core philosophy:


"The world already knows how to move experts.
RELAY helps us move expertise."


The ultimate question RELAY answers is:


"Can the organization operate without the expert?"


==================================================
THE RELAY MODEL
==================================================


RELAY structures knowledge transfer through five connected layers:


1. OPERATING MODEL
2. TRANSFER BLUEPRINT
3. TRANSFER PLAN / TRANSFER RAILS
4. KNOWLEDGE & EVIDENCE CAPTURE
5. READINESS


The product should visually reinforce this progression.


OPERATING MODEL
      ↓
TRANSFER BLUEPRINT
      ↓
TRANSFER PLAN
      ↓
REAL WORK + TRANSFER SESSIONS
      ↓
KNOWLEDGE & EVIDENCE
      ↓
READINESS


==================================================
1. OPERATING MODEL
==================================================


Knowledge transfer cannot begin with generic training.


The organization first needs to define:


"What needs to continue working after the expert leaves?"


RELAY helps the expert and host organization construct a lightweight TARGET OPERATING MODEL.


Use the following operating-model dimensions:


PROCESSES


What work must be performed?


PEOPLE & ROLES


Who performs the work?
Who owns each activity?


GOVERNANCE


What meetings, approvals, escalations, and management routines exist?


TOOLS & SYSTEMS


What systems, spreadsheets, applications, forms, and tools support the work?


DATA & METRICS


What information is required?
What KPIs are monitored?


DECISION RIGHTS


Who makes which decisions?
Which decisions currently depend on the expert?


EXTERNAL RELATIONSHIPS


Which suppliers, government entities, clinics, partners, vendors, or other external relationships matter?


CAPABILITIES


What must people actually know how to do?


==================================================
AI-GUIDED OPERATING MODEL BUILDER
==================================================


Create an AI-guided Operating Model Builder.


The AI should help the expert/program manager build the operating model through structured conversation.


For example:


"Walk me through how medicine moves from forecast to clinic."


Based on the answer, the AI should ask targeted follow-ups such as:


"Who approves an emergency order?"


"What happens when reported inventory appears incorrect?"


"Which decisions currently depend specifically on you?"


"Who do you contact when the standard process fails?"


"What tools or spreadsheets are required?"


"How do you know whether the process is working?"


The AI should convert answers into structured operating-model components.


Users must be able to:


APPROVE
EDIT
DELETE
ADD MANUALLY


The result should be a visual Operating Model.

==================================================
FORMAL VS INFORMAL KNOWLEDGE
==================================================


This distinction is central to RELAY.


FORMAL KNOWLEDGE is documented.


Examples:


SOPs
policies
training manuals
process maps
forms
system instructions


INFORMAL KNOWLEDGE is experiential.


Examples:


judgment
heuristics
exceptions
relationships
workarounds
failure modes
context
warning signs
"how things actually work"


RELAY should actively identify gaps between the two.


Example:


FORMAL RULE


Reorder medication when inventory reaches 14 days.


EXPERT PRACTICE


The expert begins evaluating replenishment earlier when:


- demand is accelerating;
- rainy season is beginning;
- inventory data is stale;
- supplier lead times are increasing.


RELAY should flag:


POTENTIAL FORMAL / INFORMAL KNOWLEDGE GAP


and offer:


CAPTURE INSIGHT
EDIT
IGNORE

==================================================
TRANSFER RAILS
==================================================


RELAY should guide capability development through structured experiential stages.


Use:


1. KNOW


Understand the formal process or concept.


2. OBSERVE


Watch the expert perform it.


3. EXPLAIN


Explain why the expert made the decisions they made.


4. ASSIST


Perform part of the work with the expert.


5. LEAD


Perform the work while the expert observes.


6. VALIDATE


Expert confirms independent capability.


7. TEACH


Local employee successfully teaches another employee.


Visually show progression through these rails.


Do NOT make this feel like an LMS or online course platform.


The emphasis is REAL WORK.


==================================================
CAPABILITY LEVELS
==================================================


Use:


0 — Not Exposed
1 — Observed
2 — Assisted
3 — Performed with Supervision
4 — Performed Independently
5 — Demonstrated Repeatedly
6 — Can Teach Others


AI may recommend progression.


AI NEVER automatically changes validated capability status.


Human validation is required.

==================================================
EXPERT DEBRIEF
==================================================


After the session, AI asks the expert 1-3 TARGETED questions.


Questions should depend on what happened.


Do not ask generic surveys.


Example:


"You decided the standard 14-day reorder threshold was not appropriate in this case. What factors caused you to override the normal rule?"


Another:


"Was there anything you noticed that Kwame did not?"


Another:


"What would an inexperienced person most likely get wrong in this situation?"


This is how RELAY extracts tacit knowledge.


==================================================
LEARNER DEBRIEF
==================================================


RELAY separately asks the learner 1-3 questions.


The purpose is to determine whether the learner actually understood the expert's reasoning.


Example:


"Why was 18 days of inventory concerning even though the reorder threshold is 14 days?"


Follow-up:


"What would you do if redistribution from another clinic were not possible?"


This provides direct evidence of knowledge transfer.


Do NOT assume that exposure means understanding.

==================================================
CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
==================================================


Keep these principles visible throughout development.


1.


RELAY does not replace experts.


It helps experts transfer what they know.


2.


RELAY does not assume that training equals capability.


It looks for evidence that the receiving employee can actually perform the work.


3.


RELAY does not assume that documentation equals knowledge transfer.


Formal documentation is only one component.


Tacit knowledge, judgment, relationships, exceptions, and real-world experience matter.


4.


RELAY does not treat knowledge transfer as an end-of-assignment activity.


Knowledge transfer should happen continuously while real work is being performed.


5.


RELAY does not make autonomous competency decisions.


AI identifies evidence and makes recommendations.


Humans validate.


6.


RELAY does not optimize primarily for course completion.


It optimizes for organizational independence.


The ultimate question is:


"Can this organization operate effectively after the expert leaves?"


7.


RELAY should create local trainers, not permanent dependence on RELAY or the original expert.


The desired outcome is:


EXTERNAL EXPERT
      ↓
LOCAL EXPERT
      ↓
LOCAL TRAINER
      ↓
LOCAL WORKFORCE


8.


Every important AI recommendation should be explainable.


The user should be able to understand:


WHY did RELAY recommend this?


WHAT evidence supports it?


WHAT operating-model component does it affect?


WHAT happens if the knowledge does not transfer?

EXPERT DEBRIEF — DETAILED UX
==================================================


The expert debrief must be extremely lightweight.


Experts should not feel like RELAY creates administrative work.


Target completion:


1-3 minutes.


AI should ask no more than approximately 3 questions unless the user chooses to continue.


Questions should be dynamically generated from the session.


Good questions:


"You chose not to trust the inventory report. What made you question it?"


"What would someone with less experience have missed here?"


"You intervened when Kwame recommended transferring 40 units. What was wrong with his reasoning?"


"Was there anything you did today that isn't described in the formal procedure?"


Bad questions:


"What did you learn today?"


"How was the session?"


"Rate the training from 1-5."


Avoid generic survey behavior.

"What information would make you decide against redistribution?"


"What would you do differently if supplier lead time returned to normal?"


"What part of today's decision would you be least comfortable making alone?"


This can identify:


UNDERSTANDING


MISUNDERSTANDING


CONFIDENCE GAP


EXPERIENCE GAP


==================================================
KNOWLEDGE CAPTURE ENGINE
==================================================


RELAY should distinguish several types of tacit knowledge.


HEURISTIC


A practical rule of thumb.


Example:


"During rainy season, don't interpret the standard reorder threshold without checking recent consumption."


EXCEPTION


When the normal process should not be followed.


Example:


"Do not redistribute even apparent surplus when the sending clinic's inventory count is stale."


WARNING SIGN


Something an experienced person notices.


Example:


"Repeated identical weekly consumption figures may indicate reporting quality problems."


RELATIONSHIP KNOWLEDGE


Who helps solve a problem.


Example:


"For urgent inter-clinic transfers, contact the regional logistics coordinator before escalating to procurement."


FAILURE MODE


A common way the process goes wrong.


Example:


"Teams often calculate surplus without considering the sending clinic's upcoming demand."


CONTEXT


Situational information affecting decisions.


Example:


"Rainy season materially changes antimalarial consumption."


LESSON LEARNED


Knowledge derived from a past case.


Each should use an appropriate structured template.
